Abstract

Few-step flow-map generators, such as MeanFlow and consistency models, sample in a few long-range jumps. On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on states drawn from its own sampler, but existing methods compare teacher and student through the local stochastic transitions of a multi-step sampler, which a flow-map student never deploys. We present FlowMap-OPD, the first systematic study of OPD for flow-map students. It separates state collection from supervision: the student collects states with its native few-step sampler, while the teacher supervises the flow map, velocities induced from it, or the instantaneous velocity linked to the map by a separately weighted consistency penalty. We find that instantaneous-velocity supervision with a small consistency weight, our chosen form, transfers the teacher's gains best, whereas supervising the deployed map, although the natural choice, transfers them worst when teacher and student start from different pretrained models. This gives a counter-intuitive rule: sample with the flow map, learn the velocity. As a further benefit, this form applies to any teacher that defines a velocity, including multi-step ones, and to students initialized from multi-step models. On FLUX and SD3.5, a single few-step student consolidates three specialist teachers within 300 updates, matches or exceeds each on its own benchmark (e.g., OCR 0.883 versus 0.850 on FLUX), and outperforms both multi-reward reinforcement learning and LoRA merging.

Method

FlowMap-OPD separates the rollout from the supervised quantity.

Method details

Rollout–kernel separation

The OPD objective is a sum of local comparisons, so its expectation depends on the rollout only through the state marginals at the comparison times. The rollout can therefore collect states with the student's native few-step sampler, while a separately chosen kernel compares student and teacher there. The kernel that compares student and teacher need not be the transition that collected the states.

Decoupled velocity supervision

A flow-map network offers three quantities to compare: the flow map, velocities induced from it, and the instantaneous velocity. FlowMap-OPD matches the teacher's instantaneous velocity and links it to the map by a consistency penalty with its own weight \(\lambda_c\). The penalty propagates the supervision of the instantaneous velocity to the long-range map the student samples with.

Only decoupled velocity supervision weights consistency separately. All are Gaussian-kernel KLs.

What rollout–kernel separation provides

  • Training rollouts are short. A \(K=4\) rollout takes five network evaluations, and students trained with \(K=3\) or \(K=2\) rollouts match the \(K=4\) student.
  • Any teacher with a velocity. With the public multi-step Flow-GRPO specialists as teachers, FlowMap-OPD trains a few-step student with five evaluations per rollout; Flow-OPD with the same teachers spends twenty.
  • Students from multi-step models. Initialized from the multi-step SD3.5-Medium weights with an added second time input, the student comes close to the few-step student in 300 updates.
